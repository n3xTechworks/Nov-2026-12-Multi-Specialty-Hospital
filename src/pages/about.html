Our story

From a 60-bed clinic to a regional referral hospital

Northcrest began in 1998 when three Bengaluru doctors opened a 60-bed hospital in Hebbal with one promise: no patient leaves without understanding their diagnosis. That promise still shapes how we work.

Today 120+ consultants, 900 nurses and 1,400 staff care for more than 2,40,000 patients a year, from routine check-ups to heart surgery and newborn intensive care.

  • Not-for-profit trust: surplus is reinvested in care and training
  • Teaching hospital for nursing and postgraduate medical courses
  • Transparent package pricing, shared before admission

Milestones

Twenty-eight years of growth

Each step added a service our community was travelling across the city to find.

  1. 1998

    Northcrest opens in Hebbal

    A 60-bed hospital with general medicine, surgery, obstetrics and a 24-hour casualty.

  2. 2004

    Heart Institute and cath lab

    The first 24/7 primary angioplasty service in north Bengaluru.

  3. 2010

    Block B and neurosciences

    200 more beds, a stroke unit, neuro ICU and the region's first 3T MRI.

  4. 2015

    Women and Children's wing

    Maternity suites, a level III NICU and a paediatric emergency room.

  5. 2019

    Cancer centre

    Medical, surgical and radiation oncology with a 30-chair day-care unit.

  6. 2024

    Robotic surgery and patient portal

    Two robotic systems and online booking, reports, prescriptions and bill payment.

Technology

Advanced equipment, used where it helps

We invest in technology that shortens recovery, sharpens diagnosis or makes treatment safer, and we train our teams before we switch it on.

  • Two robotic surgery systems for urology, gynaecology and joints
  • 3T MRI, 256-slice CT and PET-CT, open 24/7
  • Bi-plane cath lab and hybrid operating theatre
  • Electronic records shared across every department and the patient portal

Community

Health care beyond our walls

Every month our teams run free screening camps in villages and urban settlements around Bengaluru, and train first responders in CPR.

  • 120+ free health camps and 18,000 screenings last year
  • Subsidised surgery fund for families below the poverty line
  • CPR training for 5,000 school teachers and drivers
