Healthy eating on an Indian plate

Simple swaps for everyday dal, roti and rice meals that cut salt, sugar and oil without losing flavour.

Ritu MalhotraChief Clinical Dietitian
1 Oct 2026 · 6 min read

Indian food can be one of the healthiest diets in the world: it is rich in pulses, vegetables, spices and fermented foods. The trouble usually lies in portion sizes, refined grains, extra oil and hidden salt. A few steady changes make a big difference.

Build a balanced plate

At lunch and dinner, aim for half a plate of vegetables (sabzi, salad or raita), a quarter of protein (dal, chana, rajma, paneer, eggs, fish or chicken) and a quarter of whole grains. Add a small bowl of curd for gut health and a piece of whole fruit rather than juice.

Smart everyday swaps

Healthy swaps for common foods
Instead ofTry
White rice at every mealHalf rice, half millets such as ragi, jowar or foxtail
Maida parathas and naanWhole wheat phulkas or multigrain rotis
Deep-fried samosas and pakorasRoasted chana, sprouts chaat or air-fried snacks
Sweetened chai three times a dayOne cup with less sugar, plus buttermilk or lemon water
Packaged namkeenA handful of unsalted nuts or makhana

Cut back on salt and sugar

Most of our salt comes from pickles, papads, sauces, bakery items and restaurant meals rather than the salt shaker. Season with lemon, amchur, herbs and roasted spices instead. Keep added sugar below six teaspoons a day for adults, and watch for sugar in biscuits, breakfast cereals and flavoured yoghurts.

Use oil, do not pour it

Measure oil with a teaspoon. Rotate between mustard, groundnut, rice bran and sesame oils for a good mix of fats, and keep ghee to a teaspoon a day. Never reuse oil for deep frying, as reheating creates harmful compounds.

Read the label in 30 seconds

  • Check the serving size first; many packs contain two or three servings.
  • Look for sodium below 120 mg per 100 g for a low-salt choice.
  • Pick products with fibre above 3 g per 100 g.
  • Avoid anything listing hydrogenated or partially hydrogenated fat.

This article is general information. For a personal meal plan, book a session with a Northcrest dietitian.

Key points

  • Keep salt to one level teaspoon a day, across all meals.
  • Swap half your white rice or maida for millets, whole wheat or brown rice.
  • Measure oil with a spoon: about 3 to 4 teaspoons per person per day.
