Healthy ageing: staying strong after 60

Strength, balance, the right screenings and vaccines, and a yearly medicines review to stay independent for longer.

Dr Usha NatarajanConsultant Geriatrician
17 Sep 2026 · 7 min read

Growing older does not have to mean slowing down. Many of the problems we link with ageing, such as weakness, falls and confusion from medicines, can be prevented or delayed. Here is what we recommend to every patient over 60 at our healthy ageing clinic.

Keep moving and build strength

Muscle mass falls by about 1% a year after 50 unless you use it. Aim for 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, such as brisk walking, plus two strength sessions: sit-to-stands from a chair, wall push-ups, or light weights and resistance bands. Yoga and tai chi are excellent for balance and flexibility.

Prevent falls at home

  • Install grab bars in the bathroom and use non-slip mats.
  • Light the path from bed to bathroom with a night light.
  • Remove loose rugs and clutter from walkways.
  • Wear well-fitting footwear with a firm grip, indoors too.
  • Have your eyes checked every year and update glasses promptly.

Screenings and vaccines after 60

Recommended checks for adults over 60
CheckHow often
Blood pressure, sugar and cholesterolEvery year
Eyes (cataract, glaucoma) and hearingEvery year
Bone density (DEXA scan)Women from 65, men from 70, or earlier after a fracture
Bowel cancer screeningAs advised, up to age 75
Influenza vaccineEvery year, before winter
Pneumococcal and shingles vaccinesOnce, as advised by your doctor

Review your medicines every year

Many older adults take five or more medicines, and the risk of side effects, dizziness and falls rises with each one. Bring every tablet, syrup, inhaler and supplement you take to an annual review. Often one or two can be reduced or stopped safely.

Keep your mind and social life active

Learning new skills, reading, puzzles and music all help keep the brain sharp. Staying connected matters just as much: loneliness is as harmful to health as smoking. Join a walking group, volunteer or simply plan a regular call with family. If you or your family notice memory changes that affect daily life, book a memory assessment; early diagnosis opens up more options.

This article is general information and does not replace advice from your own doctor.

Key points

  • Strength and balance exercises twice a week cut falls by about a quarter.
  • Flu, pneumococcal and shingles vaccines are recommended after 60.
  • Review every medicine with your doctor at least once a year.
