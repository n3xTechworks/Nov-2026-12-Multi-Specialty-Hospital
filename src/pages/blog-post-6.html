Sleep and mental health: how they shape each other

Why poor sleep and low mood feed each other, a practical sleep routine that works, and when it is time to talk to someone.

Dr Karan SethiConsultant Psychiatrist and Sleep Specialist
24 Sep 2026 · 7 min read

Sleep and mental health are closely linked. A few bad nights can make anyone irritable and anxious, and long-term insomnia roughly doubles the risk of developing depression. The reverse is also true: anxiety and low mood often show up first as trouble falling or staying asleep. Improving one usually helps the other.

How much sleep do you need?

Most adults need seven to nine hours, teenagers eight to ten, and older adults seven to eight. The best sign you are getting enough is waking up without an alarm most days and staying alert until the evening.

A sleep routine that works

  1. Fix your wake-up time. Get up at the same time every day, even after a poor night. This is the single most powerful habit.
  2. Get morning light. Spend 15 minutes outdoors soon after waking to set your body clock.
  3. Cut caffeine after 2 pm. That includes tea, coffee, cola and energy drinks.
  4. Wind down for an hour. Dim the lights, put phones away and try reading, gentle stretching or slow breathing.
  5. Use the bed only for sleep. If you cannot sleep after about 20 minutes, get up, do something calm in dim light and return when sleepy.

When worries keep you awake

Set aside 15 minutes in the early evening as a "worry time". Write down what is on your mind and one small next step for each item. When the same thoughts return at night, remind yourself they are already on paper. Breathing slowly, in for four counts and out for six, calms the stress response within a few minutes.

When to seek help

  • Trouble sleeping on three or more nights a week for more than a month
  • Loud snoring with pauses in breathing, or falling asleep while driving
  • Low mood, loss of interest or constant worry for more than two weeks
  • Any thoughts of self-harm: please seek help today

Cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) works better than sleeping tablets in the long run, and is available at our sleep clinic. If you are in distress, the national Tele-MANAS helpline on 14416 is free and open 24 hours, or call our emergency line on +91 80 4718 1000.

This article is general information and does not replace advice from your own doctor.

Key points

  • Most adults need seven to nine hours of sleep a night.
  • Keep the same wake-up time every day, including weekends.
  • Low mood or poor sleep lasting more than two weeks deserves a conversation with a doctor.
