Pregnancy care, trimester by trimester

The scans, tests, supplements and warning signs to know at each stage, from the first positive test to your due date.

Dr Pooja GuptaConsultant Obstetrician and Gynaecologist
15 Oct 2026 · 8 min read

Pregnancy is a healthy, natural process, and regular check-ups help keep it that way. Most women need eight or more antenatal visits. Here is what to expect, stage by stage.

Before and in early pregnancy

Start folic acid 400 micrograms a day, ideally three months before you try to conceive and through the first 12 weeks. It lowers the risk of spinal defects in the baby. If you have diabetes, thyroid disease, epilepsy or high blood pressure, see your doctor before pregnancy so medicines can be reviewed.

First trimester: weeks 1 to 13

  • First visit: blood group, haemoglobin, thyroid, blood sugar, urine and screening for HIV, hepatitis B and syphilis.
  • Dating scan at 6 to 9 weeks to confirm the heartbeat and due date.
  • NT scan and double marker at 11 to 14 weeks to screen for chromosomal conditions.

Nausea, tiredness and tender breasts are common. Small, frequent meals, ginger and plenty of fluids help. Vomiting that stops you keeping any fluids down needs medical attention.

Second trimester: weeks 14 to 27

  • Anomaly scan at 18 to 22 weeks to look at the baby's organs and growth.
  • Glucose tolerance test at 24 to 28 weeks to check for gestational diabetes.
  • Iron and calcium tablets daily from around 14 to 16 weeks, as advised.
  • Td vaccine (tetanus and diphtheria), usually two doses a month apart.

Most women feel their energy return. You will start to feel the baby move between 18 and 22 weeks.

Third trimester: weeks 28 to 40

Visits become more frequent: every two weeks until 36 weeks, then weekly. We check blood pressure, urine, the baby's growth and position, and may suggest a growth scan. This is the time to write your birth plan, pack your hospital bag and take a tour of the Northcrest birthing suite.

Warning signs: call us the same day

  • Any bleeding from the vagina, or a gush of fluid
  • Severe headache, blurred vision or swelling of the face and hands
  • Fewer baby movements than usual after 28 weeks
  • Severe tummy pain, fever, or burning when passing urine
  • Regular painful contractions before 37 weeks

Our labour ward is open 24 hours. Call the emergency line on +91 80 4718 1000 and come straight in.

This article is general information and does not replace advice from your own doctor.

Key points

  • Start folic acid 400 micrograms ideally three months before pregnancy.
  • Key scans: dating, NT at 11 to 14 weeks and anomaly at 18 to 22 weeks.
  • Bleeding, severe headache or reduced baby movements need same-day review.
