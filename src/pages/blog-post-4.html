Your child's vaccination schedule in India

A clear, age-by-age guide to the vaccines your child needs from birth to 12 years, based on the national programme and paediatric guidance.

Dr Aditya JoshiConsultant Paediatrician
8 Oct 2026 · 7 min read

Vaccines are the safest, simplest way to protect your child from serious infections such as measles, whooping cough, pneumonia and polio. The schedule below combines India's Universal Immunisation Programme (UIP), whose vaccines are free at government centres, with additional vaccines recommended by paediatricians. Your paediatrician may adjust the timing for your child.

Making vaccine visits easier

  • Bring your child's vaccination card to every visit so nothing is missed.
  • Breastfeed or cuddle your baby during and after the injection; it really does ease the pain.
  • Combination vaccines can reduce the number of injections. Ask us which options suit your child.
  • Stay for 15 minutes after vaccination so we can watch for any rare reaction.

After the vaccine

Mild fever, fussiness and a sore or red spot at the injection site are common and settle within one to two days. Paracetamol in the dose your paediatrician advises can help. Call us if your baby has a fever above 39 °C, cries inconsolably for more than three hours, or seems unusually drowsy.

What if a dose was missed?

Do not worry. For almost every vaccine you simply continue from where you left off; there is no need to restart the course. Book a catch-up visit and we will draw up a plan.

This article is general information. Your paediatrician will confirm the right schedule for your child.

Key points

  • Most essential vaccines are given in the first two years.
  • Mild fever and soreness after a vaccine are normal and settle in a day or two.
  • A missed dose can usually be caught up: you rarely need to restart.
