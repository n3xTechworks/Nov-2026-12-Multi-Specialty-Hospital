Heart health after 30: the numbers that matter

Indians tend to develop heart disease about ten years earlier than people in the West. Here are the five numbers to know and the warning signs never to ignore.

Dr Sunita BhattSenior Consultant, Interventional Cardiology
29 Oct 2026 · 7 min read

Heart disease is the leading cause of death in India, and it often starts quietly. Many of the people we treat for a first heart attack in their forties had no symptoms the week before. The good news is that most heart disease is driven by a handful of risk factors you can measure, track and change. Start with the numbers.

The five numbers to know

Heart health targets for most adults
MeasureHealthy targetHow often to check
Blood pressureBelow 130/80 mmHgEvery year, or at every visit if raised
LDL cholesterolBelow 100 mg/dL (lower if you have diabetes or heart disease)Every 3 to 5 years from age 30
Fasting blood sugarBelow 100 mg/dL; HbA1c below 5.7%Every 3 years, yearly if overweight
Waist sizeBelow 90 cm for men, below 80 cm for womenEvery few months at home
Resting pulse60 to 100 beats a minute, regularWhenever you feel unwell or dizzy

The waist targets for South Asians are lower than international ones because we tend to store fat around the organs, which raises risk even at a normal body weight.

Warning signs of a heart attack

Classic chest pain feels like pressure, squeezing or heaviness in the centre of the chest that lasts more than a few minutes or comes and goes. It may spread to the left arm, jaw, neck or back. Women, older adults and people with diabetes often have less typical signs: breathlessness, sweating, nausea, unusual tiredness or a feeling that something is very wrong.

If symptoms last more than five minutes, call an ambulance. Do not drive yourself to hospital, and do not wait to see if it settles.

While you wait, sit down, loosen tight clothing and, if you are not allergic, chew one 300 mg aspirin. Every minute matters: heart muscle saved in the first hour is muscle you keep for life.

Everyday habits that protect your heart

  • Move every day. Aim for 150 minutes of brisk walking, cycling or swimming a week, plus two short strength sessions.
  • Cut the salt. Keep total salt below one level teaspoon (5 g) a day. Pickles, papads, namkeen and restaurant food hide most of it.
  • Choose fats wisely. Use mustard, groundnut or rice bran oil in small amounts, and avoid reused frying oil and vanaspati.
  • Stop tobacco in every form. Smoking, bidis and gutka all double heart-attack risk. Our quit clinic offers free counselling.
  • Sleep and stress. Seven hours of sleep and a daily wind-down routine help keep blood pressure in check.

When to book a heart check-up

Book a review if you are over 30 with a family history of early heart disease, if you have diabetes, high blood pressure or high cholesterol, or if you notice breathlessness or chest discomfort on exertion. A basic heart check at Northcrest includes an ECG, lipid profile, HbA1c and an echocardiogram if needed, and takes about two hours.

This article is general information and does not replace advice from your own doctor.

Key points

  • Know five numbers: blood pressure, LDL cholesterol, blood sugar, waist and resting pulse.
  • Chest discomfort lasting more than five minutes is an emergency: call 108 or our 24/7 emergency line.
  • Thirty minutes of brisk walking on most days lowers heart risk by about a third.
